about

Visual identity designer

Hi, I’m Pallavi, a visual identity designer who also writes. By day I work in brand strategy at PHI Learning, an academic publisher, so one design usually has to work as a catalogue cover, a social tile and a printed flyer.

I start with the idea: what a brand is trying to say and what someone should feel in the first second. I know the Adobe Creative Suite, but right now I’m working in Inkscape, a free alternative that’s just as great. I also design in Figma and use vibe coding to prototype fast.

My taste runs modern, clean and minimal, with something quirky tucked in. Outside work I keep a blog on literature, media and modern life. If that sounds like your kind of brief, say hello.

how i approach design

🎨 colour psychology firstColour changes how people feel before they read a word. It makes a bigger difference than most people expect, so I start there.
🧼 modernise everythingI like modern, clean, minimalist design: clear communication with bold ambitions.
🧪 be experimentalI love being unique. There will always be something quirky or avant-garde in my work, because that’s the style I believe in.
🧩 reusable assets, not one-offsMy designs aren’t a single piece. They already lay the foundation of a design system.
💭 purpose, with emotionA goal matters, but people aren’t always logical. I tend to follow intuition over logic.
✏️ two drafts, alwaysDraft 1 is just the skeleton: a rough direction to show you where I’m heading before the main first draft. That way we’re all on the same page.

Parley · fictional brand mark

2 pieces

Parley: a made-up podcast network for long, unhurried conversations.

I invented the brand first so the logo had a job to do: sound like a conversation, look like a letter, and shrink to an app icon.

fictional brandpodcast identitylogo design

🔊 Arcs as sound travelling

The rings spread out from the bowl of the P like sound leaving a speaker, so the mark reads as audio without drawing a microphone or headphones.

🪑 The gap is an open seat

The outer arc is deliberately cut at both ends so the shape never closes. It’s a small invitation for the listener to pull up a chair and join in.

🌗 Two moods, one mark

Warm sunset tones keep it friendly on covers and social posts. Neon green on dark suits the player and night listening, where the green doubles as a “live” signal.
